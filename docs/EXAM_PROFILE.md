<two_component_pattern>
**Every question has a technical component AND an interpretive/applied component.** Never just derivation, never just discussion. This is the strongest signal in the mock.

| Q | Technical (verb + object) | Interpretive part |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Describe the empirical evidence | supporting **AND contradicting** the model |
| 2 | Explain the model | Sketch how to numerically simulate |
| 3 | Derive (the model result) | discuss the **policy implications** |
| 4 | Derive (the Solow conditions) | give an **intuitive explanation** |

Question templates (technical + interpretive pairs) observed across the 5 past exams:

- **Describe + critique** — "Describe the evidence for and against X" *(5+ instances)*
- **Explain + sketch-sim** — "Explain X. Sketch how you would numerically simulate it" *(5+ instances — most common pair)*
- **Describe analytically and graphically + sketch-sim** — "Describe analytically and graphically the transition equation for X, and explain how you would numerically simulate it" *(2023, Undated 1 & 2 — diagram explicitly required)*
- **Shock + sketch-sim or compare** — "Consider an economy at the BGP. Suppose [parameter X] suddenly changes. Simulate / what happens?" *(2023, Undated 2)*
- **State equations + economic explanation** — "State the main equations of [model X]. Provide an economic explanation" *(Undated 1 & 2 — Romer/R&D heavy)*
- **Distinguish concepts** — "Explain the economic difference between X and Y" *(Undated 1)*
- **Imply + empirical check** — "Does [model] imply Y? Why or why not? Do empirical data support it?" *(Undated 2)*
- **Comparative statics in prose** — "How would [outcome] respond to [shock A]? And to [shock B]?" *(2023 Q4)*
- **Derive + policy** — "Derive X and discuss the policy implications" *(2019, 2020 — only on Cozzi 2017)*
- **Derive + intuition** — "Derive X and give an intuitive explanation" *(2020 only)*
- **Pure prose** — "What are the causes of X?" *(2019 — rare)*
</two_component_pattern>

<verbs_and_expectations>
- **Describe** — synthesize empirical facts in prose; no formal model required
- **Explain** — present a model: assumptions, key equations, behavior (mini-lecture in essay form)
- **Derive** — show formal mathematical reasoning step-by-step; algebra must be visible
- **Discuss** — interpret meaning, implications, real-world relevance
- **Sketch how you would [simulate/compute]** — pseudo-algorithmic; list steps, parameter choices, what to plot. Doesn't require running anything.
- **Give an intuitive explanation** — translate the math into a story a layperson could follow

Working backward from 4 × 25% over a notional 90-minute exam: ~20 min per question → expect ~400-800 words per answer, equations where relevant.
</verbs_and_expectations>

- **Topic distribution is broad and varied.** No single year covers all modules; over 5 years, M2-M6 are all live and M1 is in most years.

- **Sketch-simulation appears in nearly every exam.** This is a core skill regardless of module.

Highly emphasized in Cozzi's own lecture slides (orthogonal hotspot signal):

- **M1**: the 7 numbered stylized facts, absolute vs conditional convergence, balanced growth definition.
- **M2**: closed vs open economy autarky comparison, current account = S − I, general Solow steady state with technological progress.
- **M3**: MRW empirical motivation (rate-of-convergence + investment-rate-effect puzzles), α+ϕ vs α, Solow with land/oil and the three drags formula.
- **M4**: difference between externality-based and R&D-based endogenous growth, "scale effect" as the empirically failing prediction, the main Romer equations.
- **M5**: Cozzi (2017) hybrid model — three "Summing Up" slides in Lecture 5b. Threshold ̄n. "Policy could make policy itself more effective for growth!"

<grading_rubric>
From a student-to-student tip (worth heeding, not formally published):

**Scale appears to be /6 per question.** Pass = 4/6; top mark = 6/6.

**To pass (4/6):** the main equations + verbal reasoning are enough. A graph/phase diagram helps.

**To ace (6/6):** the answer must explain the *economics behind* the model, not just the equations. Writing down all the math without intuition caps the grade. **The intuition is the differentiator between passing and acing.**

**Canonical answer structure Cozzi expects:**

1. **Introduction** — why we should care about this model, what question it answers (1 short paragraph)
2. **Sketch the model** — assumptions and key equations (the technical half)
3. **Consequences of the model** — what it implies, comparative statics, predictions
4. **Main points / intuition** — the economic story; include a diagram/phase diagram if applicable; "why would the economy move from one point to another"

Use this 4-part structure in every model answer. The diagram is not decoration — it is graded content.

**Study advice corollary:** don't memorize results — derive them with logic. Same advice the user has internalized via the `/exercise` compound-problem design.
</grading_rubric>
