<two_component_pattern>
**Every question has a technical component AND an interpretive/applied component.** Never just derivation, never just discussion. This is the strongest signal in the mock.

| Q | Technical (verb + object) | Interpretive part |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Describe the empirical evidence | supporting **AND contradicting** the model |
| 2 | Explain the model | Sketch how to numerically simulate |
| 3 | Derive (the model result) | discuss the **policy implications** |
| 4 | Derive (the Solow conditions) | give an **intuitive explanation** |

Question templates (technical + interpretive pairs) observable from the mock plus one plausible extension:

- **Describe + critique** — "Describe the evidence for and against X"
- **Explain + applied** — "Explain X. Sketch how you would [simulate/compute/estimate] it"
- **Derive + policy** — "Derive X and discuss the policy implications"
- **Derive + intuition** — "Derive X and give an intuitive explanation"
- (Plausible, not in mock) **Compare + judge** — "Compare X and Y. Which better explains [empirical phenomenon]?"
</two_component_pattern>

<verbs_and_expectations>
- **Describe** — synthesize empirical facts in prose; no formal model required
- **Explain** — present a model: assumptions, key equations, behavior (mini-lecture in essay form)
- **Derive** — show formal mathematical reasoning step-by-step; algebra must be visible
- **Discuss** — interpret meaning, implications, real-world relevance
- **Sketch how you would [simulate/compute]** — pseudo-algorithmic; list steps, parameter choices, what to plot. Doesn't require running anything.
- **Give an intuitive explanation** — translate the math into a story a layperson could follow

Working backward from 4 × 25% over a notional 90-minute exam: ~20 min per question → expect ~400-800 words per answer, equations where relevant.
</verbs_and_expectations>

<topic_coverage_and_hotspots>
The mock covered modules **1, 3, 5, 6**. Modules 2 and 4 were NOT tested. **Do not downweight them** — N=1, and Cozzi's lectures spend equal effort on these.

Highly emphasized in Cozzi's own lecture slides (strong exam hot-spot candidates):

- **M1**: the 7 numbered stylized facts, absolute vs conditional convergence, balanced growth definition.
- **M2**: closed vs open economy autarky comparison, current account = S − I, general Solow steady state with technological progress.
- **M3**: MRW empirical motivation (rate-of-convergence + investment-rate-effect puzzles), α+ϕ vs α, Solow with oil.
- **M4**: difference between externality-based and R&D-based endogenous growth, "scale effect" as the empirically failing prediction.
- **M5**: Cozzi (2017) hybrid model — three "Summing Up" slides in Lecture 5b. Threshold ̄n. "Policy could make policy itself more effective for growth!"
- **M6**: the named **Solow condition** in efficiency wages (Lecture 6b p6: *elasticity of productivity w.r.t. real wage = 1*). Numbered "MODEL IMPLICATIONS" list (Lecture 6b p11).

**Cozzi self-cites heavily** in Module 5 (Cozzi 2007, 2017a, 2017b in Economics Letters). For that module there is no textbook chapter — professor-specific knowledge is essential.
</topic_coverage_and_hotspots>
