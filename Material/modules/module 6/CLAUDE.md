# Module 6 — Structural Unemployment

Maps to **Lecture 6** in the course plan. Covers textbook **Chapters 10 and 11**.

## Files in this module

- `Lecture 6a.md` — Chapter 10: facts and introductory theory about unemployment. Distinguishes natural/structural unemployment from short-run fluctuations; involuntary unemployment as social waste (MRS < W/P, profit-maximization implies W/P ≤ MP).
- `Lecture6b.md` — Chapter 11: efficiency wages and unemployment. Labour productivity depends positively on real wages (efficiency wage hypothesis); generates equilibrium unemployment even in an otherwise competitive market. Note: filename `Lecture6b.md` (no space) differs from the `Lecture 6a.md` convention.
- `Slides_Session6.md` — Exercise session by Maria Bolboaca: unemployment stylized facts (Exercise 6.1) and the efficiency wage model (Exercise 6.2). Includes BLS / CPS measurement methodology.

For visually-heavy pages (unemployment rate plots, labour-market diagrams), use the corresponding `.pages/p{NNN}.png` files.

## Textbook chapters

## Connections to other modules

- **Topic switch**: this is the only non-growth module. The growth models (Modules 1-5) assumed L_t = supply = demand = employment. Module 6 relaxes that and studies why involuntary unemployment exists in the long run.
- **Methodological continuity**: still long-run / structural in flavour — distinguishes from short-run business-cycle unemployment (Book Two of the textbook, out of scope for this course).
- **Exam relevance**: mock exam Q4 ("Derive the 'Solow conditions' of the efficiency wages model") maps directly to Lecture 6b / Chapter 11.
