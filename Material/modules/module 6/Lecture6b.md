- It was only for a 'clean argument' we assumed that the firm was the sole buyer in the labour market. What if there are many buying firms?
- Under traditional circumstances (without efficiency wages) this leads to wage taking behaviour. An informal argument for this goes:
- -At the going market wage each firm can get all the labour it wants.
- -Should a firm set a lower wage? Workers would seek the other firms and these would be able to absorb the additional labour because they are many. So: no!
- -Should it set a higher wage? The firm would be overflowed with labour, but it would have no interest in this since it is already able to get all the labour it wants at the lower wage. So: no!

## MODEL IMPLICATIONS

1. Possibility of truly involuntary unemployment in an equilibrium with a fully adjusted real wage
2. -Employers (not employees) drive up wages to attain an optimal level of productivity from each hired worker, and the wages thus determined (by the employers) may be so high that the employers do not want to hire all the workers who want jobs at those wages.
2. Shifts in the supply or demand shock    do not affect the real wage,    , but do affect employment,    . z * w * L
4. -Fits well with an important stylized fact of the business cycle: over the cycle, employment is much more positively correlated with GDP than real wages are.
5. -Does not fit with an important fact of growth: in the long run, real wages increase with productivity.
6. -Is the latter a problem for the efficiency wages model? No! (Exercise 6)
