## Endogenous Growth Model with R&amp;D

- Lower growth rate of the total population means that the growth rate of the labour input in the R&amp;D sector is also lower, implying convergence towards a smaller rate of technological growth in steady state.
- Lower growth of the labor force implies there is a smaller group of people who will share the capital accumulated in the past. Other things equal, this implies that ˜ k t and ˜ y t increase towards a new and higher steady state value.
- In the short run, the transitory positive growth of ˜ y t drives the positive contribution of the drop in n to g y t , but as the economy approaches the new steady state, g y t converges to the new steady state level of g t .

## Page 25

## 'Truly Endogenous' Growth ( ϕ = 1) without Scale Effect

Take-away of Lecture 5a:

- Notice that, unlike semi-endogenous growth theory, in this new truly endogenous growth theory without scale effect percapita GDP can be increasing also in the absence of population growth, ( g e = ρ s λ R ), that is when n = 0, or even with n &lt; 0 (shrinking population).
- Hence, semi-endogenous models predict that policy can affect growth only temporarily (in the transition), while truly endogenous models predict that policy can affect growth permanently.
- Therefore, according to this theory, the long-run growth rate of GDP can persistently be influenced by anything that can affect the fraction of the labour force employed in R&amp;D activities, such as R&amp;D subsidies by the government stimulating private R&amp;D investment and employment.
- Which one is right? Estimates of ϕ are close to one, which does not exclude that either of the theories is right!
