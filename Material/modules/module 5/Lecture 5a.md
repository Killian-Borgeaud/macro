## From "Endogenous" to "Truly Endogenous" growth

- In endogenous growth models we have seen so far (Chapters 8 and 9) long-term growth could be of only two types:
- semi-endogenous : policy could not a/ect long-run growth discovered by Jones ( Journal of Political Economy , 1995), Kortum ( Econometrica ,1997), and Segerstrom ( American Economic Review , 1998).
- truly endogenous with scale e/ect : larger population implied higher long-run growth - invented by Romer ( Journal of Political Economy , 1995), and Aghion and Howitt ( Econometrica , 1992)
- Now: Truly endogenous R&amp;D-driven growth model without scale e/ect:
- policy can a/ect long-run growth

## Basic Intuition - R&amp;D Dilution

- The economy has many sectors
- R&amp;D performed in one sector is often useful mainly for that sector
- Hence R&amp;D per sector is what increases productivity in the economy
- New products, new  rms, and new sectors arrive constantly
- They dilute the aggregate R&amp;D across more and more sectors
- This dilution e/ect can eliminate the scale e/ect

## Page 5

## Basic Intuition - R&amp;D Dilution

- Scale-free truly endogenous growth theory claims that the number of sectors is proportional to population
- Hence R&amp;D per sector follows R&amp;D employment as a fraction of the labour force
- R&amp;D employment as a fraction of the workforce is what causes productivity growth rate in the whole economy
- Small economies  Switzerland, Austria, Sweden - can in the long-run grow at the same rate as large economies   US, Japan, Germany - if they have the same fraction of the labour force employed in R&amp;D
- This theory was developed relatively recently, by Smulders and Van de Klundert ( European Economic Review, 1995), Dinopoulos and Thompson ( Journal of Economic Growth , 1998), Peretto ( Journal of Economic Growth , 1998), Young ( Journal of Political Economy , 1998), Howitt ( Journal of Political Economy , 1999)

## Summing Up I

- Two important endogenous growth theories are currently the most in uential
- Semi-endogenous without scale e/ect
- Truly endogenous without scale e/ect
- Both eliminate the prediction of the simplistic truly endogenous growth model with scale e/ect initially introduced by Paul Romer (1990), that is the one with ϕ = 1 and n = 0 we have seen in Chapter 9
- They di/er in their long-run policy implications
- Semi-endogenous predicts that policy can a/ect growth only temporarily (in the transition)
- Truly endogenous predicts that policy can a/ect growth permanently
- Both theories have strong empirical support

## Summing Up II

- Unlike the semi-endogenous, the truly endogenous without scale e/ect does not predict that growth be increasing with the population growth rate n
- It is not yet clear which of the two is "right" and which is "wrong"
- Empirical estimates of ϕ tend to be lower but close to 1, so that not it is not yet possible to exclude that either of the two theories is right.
