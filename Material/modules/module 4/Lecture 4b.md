## IDEAS AS ECONOMIC GOODS

- Ideas are like public goods : non-rival and imperfectly excludable.
- -Non-rival: The consumption or use of the good by one person does not prevent other persons from using it. As an example, consider calculus.
- Thus the cost of an idea is fixed (better: sunk ) cost in many cases.
- -Either it can be bought from an independent R&amp;D firm or developed in an internal company research department.
- -But once acquired, the use of the idea itself is costless.

## Page 5

- In order to have a private production of idea-based technological progress, it must somehow be ensured that the inventor of the idea holds monopoly power over the use of the idea.
- However, if one ensures a private production of ideas by giving monopoly in the use of ideas to the creators, then the resulting ideas will be used too little as compared to a social optimum.

## Page 6

## PUBLIC PRODUCTION OF IDEAS

- The preceding analysis could suggest that R&amp;D should be conducted by public or other non-profit enterprises.
- However, the costs of development would have to be financed by distortionary taxes .
- Furthermore, the general public may wish to have private R&amp;D activities, and be willing to live with the monopoly power necessarily associated with it, in so far as the profit motive creates strong incentives for technological development.

## Page 7

## EXCLUDABILITY AND PATENTS

- A good is excludable if the owner of the good can exclude other persons or firms from using it.
- While non-rivalry prevents private idea-based production under perfect competition, complete non-excludability prevents private production under any market structure.
- A patent gives a firm the right to be the sole user of an idea for some period.
- Thus patents work by ensuring both a large degree of excludability and the monopoly power required for a private production of ideas 7

## Page 8

## SUMMING UP

- The microeconomics associated with R&amp;D is subtle: private production of ideas requires imperfect competition and arrangements to ensure excludability such as patents.
- Public production with open access could be well motivated from a welfare viewpoint, but we may want to use the profit motive as an engine for technological progress.
- For now we disregard microeconomic subtleties and formulate a more traditional macro model: a set of growth and technology relations - including R&amp;D.
