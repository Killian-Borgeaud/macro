## Endogenous Growth Model with Productive Externalities: Motivation

- The previous Solow models discussed in class had long-run economic growth driven by technological growth.
- However, technological growth was exogenous, A t + 1 = ( 1 + g ) A t , and hence unexplained in these models.
- We further analyze different ways to endogenize the long-run technological growth rate. Therefore, we allow economic policy to influence long-run growth.
- One class of growth theories includes externalities in the accumulation of knowledge possessed by firms (physical capital) or by workers (human capital).
- The main idea is that a given firm is more productive the higher the average knowledge capital stock of other firms.

## Page 8

## The Importance of ϕ

<!-- formula-not-decoded -->

- If ϕ = 0, then we have the basic Solow model
- If ϕ &gt; 0, then we have increasing returns in the aggregate production function
- if ϕ &lt; 1, then α + ϕ ( 1 -α ) &lt; 1: diminishing returns to the reproducible factor, capital, alone. This leads to 'semi-endogenous growth'.
- if ϕ = 1, then α + ϕ ( 1 -α ) = 1: constant returns to capital alone. Leads to truly endogenous growth.

## Variables in per 'Worker' Terms

- How does the growth rate depend on the model parameters n and ϕ ?
- When ϕ &lt; 1 the exponent on 1 + n is positive. Hence, a larger population growth rate gives a higher growth rate of output per worker in the steady state. Not realistic!
- A larger ϕ means a larger impact of the productive externality, reducing the degree of diminishing returns to capital (and to capital per worker). Diminishing returns to capital cause convergence, so as diminishing returns disappear, convergence becomes increasingly slow. When ϕ = 1, the productive externality is strong enough to eliminate diminishing returns, implying no convergence at all.

## Endogenous Growth ( ϕ = 1): The AK Model

<!-- formula-not-decoded -->

- What is the effect of the change in the savings rate on the growth rate here?
- An increase in s gives a higher growth rate of output per worker.
- Truly endogenous growth: positive growth in output without exogenous technological growth or population growth; the growth rate is given by model parameters, among these s .
- Structural policies: higher s and lower δ give everlasting higher growth in GDP and consumption per capita.
- Critique - Scale effect: remember that A = L 1 -α . A larger constant population results in higher growth and an increasing population gives accelerating growth. Not realistic!
