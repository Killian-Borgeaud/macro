## CONCLUSION

- Implications for domestic structural policy intended to increase income and consumption per capita in the long run are qualitatively the same as in the Solow model of a closed economy, i.e., increase savings rate, etc. The effects of such policies are even quantitatively of similar magnitude.
- Should capital movements be liberalized? It has an unambiguously positive effect on national income per capita in the long run but ambiguous effects on factor incomes.

## Negative implications:

- -particular factor income (e.g., real wage) is sometimes affected negatively
- -the economy becomes exposed to interest rate shocks
